Ad reach & engagement data: what is actually obtainable

For the debt-settlement study — correlating advertising exposure with settlement outcomes. Compiled 28 October 2026.

Official platform's own documentation Verified live queried directly, result reproduced here Third-party vendor or blog claim, unconfirmed

The finding that reshapes the study

Neither Google nor Meta publishes impressions, likes or views for US commercial ads — through any API, at any price. This is not an access tier that can be bought into. Reach disclosure exists only where a regulator compelled it:

  • EU/EEA-delivered ads carry reach figures because the Digital Services Act requires it. Meta exposes eu_total_reach and demographic breakdowns; Google publishes impression ranges per country and platform. Both are explicitly scoped to EU delivery. Official
  • Political and issue ads carry spend and impression ranges in any market. Official
  • Everything else — which is all five US debt-settlement advertisers in this study — exposes creative, dates and page only.

The metric the study was designed around does not exist in the public record for its subjects. That is a design constraint, not a tooling problem to be solved with a better client.

Paid panel estimates

Pathmatics (Sensor Tower), Nielsen Ad Intel, MediaRadar, Semrush AdClarity, Adyntel

What they are

None of these vendors receives impression data from Meta or Google. They run consumer panels, observe which ads panelists are served, and extrapolate to population-level impressions. What you buy is a model output, not a platform figure.

What you would get

Modelled US impressions and spend at brand level — the only thing resembling the target metric. Pathmatics has an API but it is enterprise-tier on custom pricing. AdClarity is the cheapest credible entry at roughly $169/month but exposes no public API. Adyntel is API-first and priced per call, but returns ranges rather than exact figures. Third-party

The problem for this study specifically

If impressions are the independent variable and they originate in a vendor's panel model, the correlation being estimated is between that model's output and the outcome — not between observed exposure and the outcome. Vendors do not fully disclose extrapolation methodology, so the error cannot be characterised.

The sharper issue is coverage in the tail. Panel methods degrade as advertiser size falls, and four of the five companies in this study are small. At roughly 1,183 page likes, Pacific Debt Relief is very unlikely to register in any panel at all — so the data would be missing precisely where variation is needed.

When it is the right call

Budget exists, US impressions are genuinely required, and the write-up can carry “vendor-estimated” as a stated limitation that reviewers will accept.

Academic access via Meta Content Library

Free for qualifying researchers — but read the cost structure carefully

The cost structure changed in January 2026

Meta's own page states there are no fees for access or computation. The ICPSR terms, as documented by Bellingcat, say free compute on SOMAR's Virtual Data Enclave ended 31 December 2025, and from January 2026 SOMAR charges $371/month per research team plus a one-time $1,000 project-start fee for new teams. API work on Meta's own Secure Research Environment remains free. Third-party

These are two different routes selected at application time, and the choice determines whether this costs nothing or roughly $5,500 per year. Choose the Meta SRE.

Process and limits

  • Applications go through ICPSR at the University of Michigan; review typically takes 2–6 weeks. The UI and the API have separate application steps.
  • Eligibility: affiliation with an academic institution, or a not-for-profit whose core activity is scientific or public-interest research. Official
  • Retrieval is capped at 500,000 records per rolling seven days per researcher — far more than 40 companies would require. Rate limits are 60 synchronous searches/minute.

The catch to plan around

Content Library covers public Pages, posts, groups and events, with reactions, shares, views and comments. It contains no ads. What you would be measuring is organic engagement on these companies' Facebook Pages — a proxy for brand attention, defensible because its provenance is clean, but not ad exposure. A post cannot be linked to an ad, and dark-posted ads never appear at all.

Proxies from data obtainable today

Free, reproducible, and honest about what it measures

Advertising effort — already built

ad_days from the Meta pipeline and ad counts from the Google Transparency side. These measure effort, not exposure, and should be described that way.

Brand search volume — the strongest addition

The Google Ads API Keyword Planner returns monthly search volume for brand terms (“national debt relief” and so on), which is a direct attention signal and responds to advertising.

The gotcha: accounts without recent ad spend receive bucketed ranges (1K–10K) rather than exact numbers. Reports suggest $50–100 of spend lifts this within a day or two, though no official Google statement confirms that threshold. Basic API access allows 15,000 operations/day. Third-party

Do not substitute Google Trends: the official Trends API remains alpha and application-only, and pytrends is archived and rate-limited.

Site traffic — the weakest link

SimilarWeb estimates are expensive, and site traffic reflects far more than advertising. Include only if something else already justifies the subscription.

The most useful thing found — CFPB complaints Verified live

The CFPB Consumer Complaint Database API is open, requires no key, and was queried directly while compiling this note. Filtering product = Debt or credit management • Debt settlement from January 2023:

5,727 complaints across 724 companies, each carrying company, date received, state, issue and company response — already at company × month resolution.

Why this matters twice over

It supplies an outcome variable at the right resolution, and a sampling frame. That 724-company list, filtered to actual settlement firms, is the route from five companies to thirty or forty — which directly addresses the statistical problem below.

Recommended design

Advertising effortad-days (Meta), ad counts (Google)
→
Attentionbrand search volume (Keyword Planner)
→
OutcomeCFPB complaints per company-month

A company × month panel, roster expanded to roughly 40 firms drawn from the CFPB company list. Every input is free, every source documented, and the whole chain is reproducible from the scripts already in this repository.

On statistical power. Three advertisers across twelve months is not a dataset — only three of the five companies advertise on Meta at all, and the Google side currently spans a 12-month window. Forty firms across three years is a workable panel, and within-firm variation over time is the identification strategy that makes the correlation meaningful.

The honest caveat. CFPB provides complaints, not settlement amounts. Dollars settled are not available from any free API — the routes are company disclosures, AFCC industry data, state regulator filings and court records, most of which are not machine-readable. Whether complaints-as-outcome answers the research question should be settled before any further data work, because it determines the entire plan.
